# Protocol UC-04: Oort's 1927 detection of galactic rotation, rerun with Gaia

- Protocol id: `uc04-oort-1927`, version 1.0.0
- Module: `sdp_stellar.oort1927`
- Predeclared choices: [`sdp_stellar/oort1927.json`](../../python/packages/sdp-stellar/src/sdp_stellar/oort1927.json). Every run records that file's SHA-256, and a run whose file differs from the committed one says so.
- Written and committed on 2026-10-31, before any Gaia star was read for this protocol.

## The source

J. H. Oort (1927), "Observational evidence confirming Lindblad's hypothesis of a rotation of the galactic system", *Bulletin of the Astronomical Institutes of the Netherlands* 3, No. 120, pp. 275–282 (1927BAN.....3..275O). Every equation and number below was read from the scan Leigh uploaded. None was typed from memory.

Oort writes the effect of differential rotation on stars at distance r as a radial velocity `rA sin 2(l − l0)` and a transverse velocity `rA cos 2(l − l0) + rB`, with `A = (V/4R)(1 − (R/K) ∂K/∂R)`, `B = A − V/R`, and `V = √(RK)`. He names the two limits: solid-body rotation gives `A = 0` and `B = −V/R`, and all mass at the centre gives `A = +3V/4R` and `B = −V/4R`. From the radial velocities of ten groups of distant stars (his Table 2) he finds a total `A = +0.0310 ± 0.0037 km/s/pc` and a centre at longitude 323° ± 2.4° (in the galactic longitude system of 1927). Taking `V = 272 km/s` from Strömberg and the ratio of the two parts of the force, K2/K1 = 0.11, from about 600 proper motions, he puts the centre at R = 5900 pc. A note added in proof gives K2/K1 = 0.29 and 5100 pc, or 6600 pc if K2 = 0. He also finds a central mass of at least 8 × 10¹⁰ M☉.

## Research question

With Gaia DR3's proper motions and parallaxes for millions of stars, and its radial velocities, is the differential rotation Oort detected in 1927 there, and is his value of A borne out?

## Part A: the 1927 arithmetic (no data)

These are recomputed on every run and set beside the printed figures. Nothing in Part A is asserted, and a difference is reported for the reader to judge.

1. The weighted mean of the ten A values in Table 2, with weights 1/error², against the printed total 0.0310 ± 0.0037.
2. `A = r̄A × π̄` for the two Table 2 rows whose `r̄A` and mean parallax we read (B3–B5 and the faint c-stars).
3. R from `A = (3V/4R)·K1/(K1 + K2)`, which follows from his formula for A when K1 falls as R⁻² (the central mass) and K2 rises as R. This is computed for K2/K1 = 0.11, 0.29 and 0, against his 5900, 5100 and 6600 pc.
4. The central mass `M = (V²R/G)·K1/(K1 + K2)` with R = 5900 pc, against his "at least 8 × 10¹⁰".
5. The semi-amplitude `rA` at r = 1000 pc in his Keplerian example (R = 10,000 pc, V = 300 km/s, A = 3V/4R), against his "over 20 km/s".

The constants come from astropy (`G`, and κ = 1 au/yr in km/s).

## Hypotheses

| Id | Statement |
|---|---|
| UC04-H1 | The local Galactic disc rotates differentially as Oort found: A > 0 and B < 0. |
| UC04-H0 | It does not shear: A = 0, which is Oort's solid-body case. |

A second, separate comparison asks whether Gaia's value agrees with Oort's A = 31.0 ± 3.7 km/s/kpc. It is reported, and it does not change the verdict on UC04-H1.

## Inputs

- **Proper-motion method (decides):** the whole sky from the local bulk Gaia DR3 copy, in one pass over every file (`sdp oort1927 run`), or a Parquet file of native `gaia_source` columns (`--parquet`).
- **Radial-velocity method (Oort's own, reported):** the `rvs` bulk subset (`sdp archive bulk --subset rvs`, DSI-021). That subset holds the about 33.8 million stars that Gaia DR3 gives a radial velocity, according to ESA's data model page. It is skipped, with a note, until the subset is complete.

## Selection (predeclared)

- Shared cuts: `ruwe < 1.4`, `parallax / parallax_error > 10`, `duplicated_source` false, and finite proper motions and G.
- Distance `d = 1 / (ϖ − zp)` in kpc, with zp the scenario's parallax zero-point.
- Each scenario then applies its own distance shell, |b| limit, G limit and population.
- "blue" means `bp_rp < 0.5`. It is the nearest Gaia stand-in for Oort's early-type stars.

## The model

The generalised Oort constants describe the local velocity field to first order in distance. The heliocentric axes have x toward l = 0, y toward l = 90° and z toward the north galactic pole:

`v_x = (K + C)x + (A − B)y − U`, `v_y = (A + B)x + (K − C)y − V`, `v_z = −W`

Here (U, V, W) is the Sun's own motion. For circular rotation V_c(R), `A = ½(V_c/R − dV_c/dR)` and `B = −½(V_c/R + dV_c/dR)`, the same A and B as Oort's. Projected onto each star:

- longitude: `v_l = κ μ_l* d = d cos b (B + A cos 2l − C sin 2l) + U sin l − V cos l`
- latitude: `v_b = κ μ_b d = −d sin b cos b (K + A sin 2l + C cos 2l) + (U cos l + V sin l) sin b − W cos b`
- radial: `v_r = d cos²b (K + A sin 2l + C cos 2l) − (U cos l + V sin l) cos b − W sin b`

`A sin 2l + C cos 2l` is Oort's `A sin 2(l − l0)`. His A is the amplitude `√(A² + C²)` and his centre longitude is `l0 = −½ atan2(C, A)`.

- **Proper-motion method:** least squares for (U, V, W, A, B, C, K) from `v_l` and `v_b` together.
- **Radial-velocity method:** least squares for (U, V, W, A, C, K) from `v_r` alone. B does not enter a radial velocity.
- **Clipping:** a star is dropped when its residual in any component is more than 4 × (1.4826 × MAD) of that component, and the fit is repeated. This is done at most 3 times. It removes halo and runaway stars that the disc model does not describe.
- **Errors:** the sandwich covariance, clustered by star. A fit with fewer than 200 stars fails with that reason.

Units: velocities in km/s and distances in kpc, so A, B, C and K are in km/s/kpc. Oort's km/s/pc are 1000 times smaller.

## Scenario axes (predeclared)

1. Distance shell: 0.1–0.5, 0.3–1.0 and 0.5–1.5 kpc.
2. |b| limit: 15° and 30°.
3. G limit: 13 and 15.
4. Parallax zero-point: 0 and −0.017 mas.
5. Population: all, and blue (`bp_rp < 0.5`).

That is 48 scenarios for each method. The **baseline** is 0.3–1.0 kpc, |b| < 30°, G < 15, zp −0.017 mas, all stars.

## Decision rule (proper-motion method)

- **Supports UC04-H1** when A ≥ 5σ_A and B ≤ −5σ_B in every scenario, the baseline included.
- **Supports UC04-H0** when |A| < 2σ_A in every scenario.
- **Inconclusive** otherwise, or when any scenario's fit fails. A baseline-only run is a quick look and is always inconclusive.

The radial-velocity method gets the same reading on A alone, and it is reported beside the verdict.

**Comparison with Oort.** For each scenario, z = (√(A² + C²) − 31.0) / √(σ² + 3.7²).

- The comparison is `agrees_with_oort` when |z| < 2 in every scenario.
- It is `disagrees_with_oort` when |z| ≥ 2 in every scenario.
- It is `mixed` otherwise.

**Oort's distance to the centre, from Gaia's constants.** These are reported only:

- `R = 272 / (A − B)`, with Oort's V and Gaia's Ω = A − B
- R from Gaia's A with his three values of K2/K1, as in Part A

## Validation before use

The module is run on synthetic galaxies of stars on circular orbits, with V_c(R) = V_0 (R/R_0)^s, an isotropic 20 km/s dispersion, a solar motion, and the centre turned to a chosen longitude. The tests require:

- A and B within 5% of the planted rotation curve's values, for flat, falling and rising curves
- l0 within 2° of the planted centre
- the solar motion within 1 km/s
- the verdict to follow the plant: H1 for a flat curve, H0 for solid-body rotation (s = 1)
- the comparison to follow a planted A of 31 against 15 km/s/kpc

## What this is not

- It does not remeasure Oort's stars. Gaia's sample is far larger and is mostly old disc stars, whose mean motion lags rotation (asymmetric drift). That shifts the solar V term, but to first order not A or B. The blue scenarios are the nearest match to Oort's early-type groups.
- The first-order field ignores terms of order d/R_0. The shells stop at 1.5 kpc, and the synthetic tests show the size of that error.
- Oort's centre longitude, 323°, is in the galactic coordinate system of 1927. Gaia's l0 is in today's system. This protocol has no cited conversion between the two, so the two longitudes are reported side by side and not compared.

## The first run (2026-11-01)

Leigh ran 1.0.0 on the whole bulk copy, including the rvs subset, with the committed choices (SHA-256 `8370ef0e…`). The report is kept in `python/tests/cassettes/gaia-oort/`.

- **Verdict: supports UC04-H1.** All 48 scenarios detect rotation by both methods. None is null, and none failed.
- **Proper-motion baseline** (6,638,336 stars):
  - A = 15.22 ± 0.02 and B = −13.24 ± 0.02 km/s/kpc
  - C = −2.97 and K = −2.29 km/s/kpc
  - Ω = A − B = 28.46 km/s/kpc
  - l0 = 5.5° in today's longitudes
- **Radial-velocity baseline** (6,026,902 stars, Oort's own method): A = 15.15 ± 0.03 km/s/kpc.
- **Against Oort:** `disagrees_with_oort` for both methods. Gaia's amplitude is about half his 31.0 ± 3.7, at z between −3.8 and −4.4 in every scenario. The sign and the double wave he found are confirmed, and his size for A is not.
- **His distance to the centre:** Oort's own chain (V = 272 km/s and K2/K1 = 0.11) applied to Gaia's A gives R = 0.75 · 272 / 0.01522 / 1.11, about 12.1 kpc, instead of his 5.9 kpc. With Gaia's Ω, R = 272 / 28.46, about 9.6 kpc. Both take his V as given, and the protocol checks neither.

The known-answer test (STL-019) refits a fixed 26,357-star sample of this run and requires the baseline to agree within the sample's errors.
