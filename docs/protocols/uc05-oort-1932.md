# Protocol UC-05: Oort's 1932 force perpendicular to the galactic plane, rerun with Gaia

- Protocol id: `uc05-oort-1932`, version 1.0.0
- Module: `sdp_stellar.oort1932`
- Predeclared choices: [`sdp_stellar/oort1932.json`](../../python/packages/sdp-stellar/src/sdp_stellar/oort1932.json). Every run records that file's SHA-256, and a run whose file differs from the committed one says so.
- Written and committed on 2026-11-04, before any Gaia star was read for this protocol.

## The source

J. H. Oort (1932), "The force exerted by the stellar system in the direction perpendicular to the galactic plane and some related problems", *Bulletin of the Astronomical Institutes of the Netherlands* 6, No. 238, pp. 249–287 (1932BAN.....6..249O). Every equation and number below was read from the scan Leigh uploaded. None was typed from memory.

**His method (sections 2 and 5).** Stars that are "well mixed" in the z direction obey, for an arbitrary velocity distribution, his formula (7):

`K(z) = (1/Δ) ∂(Δ·Z̄²)/∂z`

Here Δ(z) is the density of a group of stars at height z, Z̄² the mean square of their velocity perpendicular to the plane, and K(z) the acceleration, counted positive toward increasing z. He takes Δ(z) from van Rhijn's counts in four groups of absolute magnitude (−1.5 to −0.5, −0.5 to +1.5, +1.5 to +3.5, +3.5 to +5.5; his Table 13), and Z̄² from radial velocities of stars between ±40° and ±90° latitude. In practical units this is his formula (9), `K(z) = 7.48·10⁻⁹ Z̄² ∂log(Z̄²Δ)/∂z`, with z in pc, Z in km/s and K in cm/s². He tentatively puts the Sun 30 pc north of the plane.

**His result (summary and Table 14).** |K(z)| rises in proportion to z from 0 to 200 pc, and from 200 to 500 pc it "remains practically constant and equal to 3·8·10⁻⁹ cm/sec²". Table 14 gives the force at z = 50, 100, 150, 200, 250, 300, 400 and 600 pc, preliminary and corrected.

**The density (section 11).** Inside a homogeneous ellipsoid of semi-axes a and c, `∂K/∂z = −4πγ x Δ` (14), with a shape factor x(a/c) (15) of 0.750, 0.860, 0.950 and 1.000 for a/c = 5, 10, 30 and ∞. From Table 14 he finds `∂K/∂z = 5.62·10⁻³⁰ sec⁻²`. After allowing for the inner parts of the system in four cases (0.108, 0.093, 0.089 and 0.079), "the most probable value of the total density near the sun is thus ·092 suns/ps³ or 6·3·10⁻²⁴ g/cm³", with a probable error of about 20%. The visible stars down to +13.5 visual absolute magnitude give 0.038 M☉/pc³ (his Table 34). The difference is his "dark matter". He sets his value beside Kapteyn's 0.099, Jeans's 0.143 and Lindblad's 0.217.

## Research question

With Gaia DR3's parallaxes for the density and its radial velocities and proper motions for the vertical velocities, does the vertical force near the Sun have the shape Oort found, and is his local density borne out?

## Part A: the 1932 arithmetic (no data)

These are recomputed on every run and set beside the printed figures. Nothing in Part A is asserted, and a difference is reported for the reader to judge.

1. The constant of formula (9): ln 10 × (1 km/s)² / 1 pc in cm/s², against his 7.48·10⁻⁹.
2. The constant in `Z₀ = √(6·17·10⁸ ∫K′dz)`: 2 × 1 pc / (1 km/s)² in his units, against 6.17·10⁸. His example "Z₀ = 30 km/s reaches 500 pc" is recomputed from Table 14's integral, interpolated linearly between 400 and 600 pc.
3. The shape factor x of formula (15) for a/c = 5, 10, 30 and ∞, against his 0.750, 0.860, 0.950 and 1.000.
4. ∂K/∂z from Table 14's mean corrected force, by a straight line through the origin over z ≤ 150 pc and over z ≤ 200 pc, against his 5.62·10⁻³⁰ s⁻².
5. The density from formula (14) with his 5.62·10⁻³⁰ and x = 1, before any allowance for the inner parts. It has no printed counterpart and is reported beside his four cases.
6. The plateau: the mean of Table 14's preliminary force over 200–400 pc, against the summary's 3.8·10⁻⁹.
7. One M☉/pc³ in g/cm³, against his 6.85·10⁻²³, and 0.092 M☉/pc³ in g/cm³, against his 6.3·10⁻²⁴.
8. The integral column of Table 14 by the trapezium rule on the mean corrected force, against the printed column. He integrated a smooth curve, so a difference is expected.

The constants come from astropy (`G`, the parsec, the solar mass).

## Hypotheses

| Id | Statement |
|---|---|
| UC05-H1 | Oort's force law holds: near the Sun \|K(z)\| rises with z and has flattened by 200–500 pc. |
| UC05-H0 | It does not flatten: over 200–500 pc \|K(z)\| still rises at least half as fast as it does near the plane. |

Two separate comparisons are reported, and neither changes the verdict:

- Gaia's local total density against Oort's 0.092 M☉/pc³.
- Gaia's force against his Table 14, point by point, and his plateau of 3.8·10⁻⁹ cm/s².

## Inputs

- **The density of each tracer group:** the whole sky from the local bulk Gaia DR3 copy, in one pass over every file (`sdp oort1932 run`), or a Parquet file of native `gaia_source` columns (`--parquet`).
- **The vertical velocities:** the `rvs` bulk subset (`sdp archive bulk --subset rvs`, DSI-021), or a Parquet file with `radial_velocity` (`--rv-parquet`). The run refuses to start without velocities, since formula (7) needs both.

The density uses the full copy, not the rvs subset, because a radial velocity is measured only for brighter stars, and the subset is not complete in distance. The velocities use the rvs subset, whose selection depends on brightness, not on velocity, so its mean square velocity at each height is fair.

## Selection (predeclared)

- **Shared cuts:** `ruwe < 1.4`, `parallax / parallax_error > 10`, `duplicated_source` false, finite G. For velocities, also a finite radial velocity and proper motion.
- **Distance:** `d = 1 / (ϖ − zp)`, with zp the scenario's parallax zero-point. Galactic position: l, b from astropy, `z_helio = d sin b` and `R_xy = d cos b`.
- **Absolute magnitude:** `M_G = G + 5 log₁₀(ϖ − zp) − 10`, with ϖ in mas. No extinction is applied. This is why stars below |b| = 20° are excluded: Oort himself finds absorption "only becomes sensible below 15° latitude". His groups are in visual magnitudes, and Gaia's G is taken for them at face value.
- **The column:** a cylinder of radius R_cyl about the Sun, perpendicular to the plane, with |b| ≥ 20°. At low |z| the |b| limit narrows it to a cone. The exact volume of each height bin is computed from that geometry.
- **The plane:** the Sun's height z☉ is estimated in each scenario as minus the median z_helio of the tracer stars within 300 pc of the Sun's height. Heights are then `z = z_helio + z☉`, folded to |z|. Oort's tentative +30 pc is reported beside it.

## The estimate

For each scenario:

1. **Bins** of width w in |z| from 0 to 1000 pc.
2. **Density** `ν_i = N_i / V_i`: the tracer count over the exact volume, both hemispheres.
3. **Vertical velocity:**
   - Each rvs star gets the heliocentric `v_z = v_r sin b + κ μ_b d cos b`.
   - In each bin the mean square about the bin's mean is taken. The mean removes the Sun's own W.
   - Optionally, stars more than 4 × (1.4826 × MAD) from the bin median are clipped first.
   - The mean squared radial-velocity error times sin²b is then subtracted. Proper-motion errors at these distances are a fraction of a km/s and are not subtracted.
4. **Bin minimums:** a bin is used only with at least 50 density stars and 30 velocity stars.
5. **Formula (7)** at the edge between two used neighbouring bins:
   - `|K| = −(P_{i+1} − P_i) / (z_{i+1} − z_i) / √(ν_i ν_{i+1})`, with `P = ν σ²` and z the bin centres.
   - Converted to cm/s².
6. **The slope near the plane** k₁: the least-squares line through the origin to |K| at the edges with z ≤ 200 pc, in s⁻² (Oort's ∂K/∂z).
7. **The flattening** f: the slope of a least-squares line to |K| at the edges with 200 ≤ z ≤ 500 pc, divided by k₁. Oort's force law has f near 0. A force still rising as fast as near the plane has f = 1. The plateau force is the mean |K| over the same edges.
8. **The local density**, from Poisson's equation in cylindrical form for a disc in circular rotation:
   - `4πGρ₀ = k₁ − 2(A² − B²)`
   - The radial term uses Gaia's own Oort constants from UC-04's full run: A = 15.22 ± 0.02 and B = −13.24 ± 0.02 km/s/kpc.
   - It is about 2% of Oort's 5.62·10⁻³⁰.
   - Oort's equivalent is his shape factor x.
   - ρ₀ is in M☉/pc³ and g/cm³.
9. **Errors:** 1000 Monte Carlo draws, seeded with 1932.
   - Each count N_i is drawn from a Poisson distribution.
   - Each σ²_i is drawn as normal, with variance `(m₄ − σ⁴) / n` from its own fourth moment.
   - Everything is recomputed, and the standard deviation over draws is the error.
10. **Failure:** a scenario fails, with the reason, when fewer than 3 edges fall at z ≤ 200 pc or fewer than 3 in 200–500 pc.

## Scenario axes (predeclared)

1. Tracer group: Oort's four absolute-magnitude groups, and all of them together (M_G from −1.5 to +5.5).
2. Cylinder radius: 150 and 300 pc.
3. Bin width: 25 and 50 pc.
4. Parallax zero-point: 0 and −0.017 mas.
5. Velocity clipping: on and off.

That is 80 scenarios. The **baseline** is all groups, 300 pc, 50 pc bins, zp −0.017 mas, clipping on.

## Decision rule

- A scenario **flattens** when k₁ ≥ 5σ and f + 2σ_f < 0.5.
- It **does not flatten** when k₁ ≥ 5σ and f − 2σ_f > 0.5.
- **Supports UC05-H1** when every scenario flattens, the baseline included.
- **Supports UC05-H0** when no scenario flattens and every scenario does not flatten.
- **Inconclusive** otherwise, or when any scenario failed. A baseline-only run is a quick look and is always inconclusive.

**Comparison with Oort's density.** His probable error of 20% is a 1σ of 0.2 × 0.092 / 0.6745 = 0.027 M☉/pc³. For each scenario, z = (ρ₀ − 0.092) / √(σ² + 0.027²).

- `agrees_with_oort` when |z| < 2 in every scenario.
- `disagrees_with_oort` when |z| ≥ 2 in every scenario.
- `mixed` otherwise.

**Also reported:**

- the force at each edge beside Table 14's mean corrected and preliminary values at the same z
- k₁ against his 5.62·10⁻³⁰, as a ratio
- the plateau against his 3.8·10⁻⁹
- ρ₀ − 0.038, Gaia's total density less Oort's own census of the visible stars. It is labelled as his census, since this protocol does not count Gaia's stars into a mass.

## Validation before use

The module is run on synthetic columns of stars. The tracers are isothermal (σ = 20 km/s at every height) in a known potential, and each column has a chosen Sun height, radial-velocity errors, and the |b| and cylinder geometry above. The tests require:

- **A self-gravitating sheet**, `K(z) = −K∞ tanh(z / z₀)` with z₀ = 150 pc:
  - k₁ within 10% of K∞ / z₀
  - ρ₀ within 10% of the planted value
  - the scenario flattens
- **A uniform slab**, `K(z) = −k z`:
  - k₁ within 10% of k
  - the scenario does not flatten
- **The Sun's height** recovered within 5 pc.
- **The radial-velocity error** subtracted, so that a planted error does not raise σ².
- **The verdict** follows the plant: H1 for the sheet and H0 for the slab.
- **The density comparison** follows a planted ρ₀ near and far from 0.092.

## What this is not

- It does not remeasure Oort's stars. His velocities come from a few hundred radial velocities and his densities from van Rhijn's counts.
- The four groups are in Gaia's G, not his visual magnitudes, and no extinction is applied.
- The radial term in Poisson's equation takes UC-04's A and B as given.
- It does not count Gaia's visible stars into a mass. That needs a mass–luminosity relation, which no source in this project gives yet. A follow-up protocol can add one when a source is uploaded.
- Most of the "dark matter" of 1932 is now known to include interstellar gas, which Oort judged "probably small". This protocol does not test that: it compares totals.
